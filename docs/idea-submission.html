Track: Multi-Tenant Platforms — Starter Kit 06, Multi-Employer Payroll Executor. Also entering Founder's Choice / Judges' Choice, plus an optional Metal Award add-on (instant stablecoin fallback rail).

One line: An AI payroll agent for global contractor and EOR platforms. It funds, converts and routes every payout so it lands before the local payday cut-off. It never pays one employer's contractors with another employer's money, it holds payouts after suspicious bank-detail changes, and it escalates only the exceptions a human must decide.

Problem

Contractor and EOR platforms pay thousands of people in dozens of countries for many employer clients. Every payday the ops team makes the same high-stakes decisions under time pressure, in spreadsheets and Slack, often at 11 pm:

  • Short or late funding. Employer funding arrives late, partially, or into the wrong place. Who gets paid now, and from whose money? Paying Employer A's contractors with Employer B's funds is a compliance breach and the classic pooled-ledger bug. Summing balances across tenants reports "enough money" for a batch the short employer cannot fund.
  • Rails and cut-offs. Every country has its own rail, fee and cut-off. Pick SWIFT where LOCAL exists and the contractor pays a fee. Miss a cut-off and wages arrive late, which damages trust and in many jurisdictions carries penalties.
  • FX and fees. Convert too little (forgetting per-payout SWIFT fees) and the last contractor's payout fails. Convert too much and you waste spread.
  • Payroll diversion fraud. "Hi, it's Maria, please send my pay to this new account today, urgent." This is one of the most common payroll scams.

Solution: the decisions the agent makes

For each employer (one Airwallex connected account per employer = hard tenant isolation) and each payroll run, Payday Guardian builds a plan and re-decides when new information arrives:

  1. Fund. Read each employer's own balances with x-on-behalf-of. Never borrow across tenants. On a shortfall, pay priority wages first (by contractor due date and jurisdiction rules), hold the rest, and escalate an exact funding request to the employer.
  2. Price. Compute payroll + per-payout SWIFT fees + the platform fee before converting, so no payout fails on insufficient funds.
  3. Convert. Net same-currency balances already held in the employer's Global Accounts against obligations. Convert only the gap, at a locked single-use FX quote, on the employer's account, within an auto-approve limit. Read balances back to confirm where it landed.
  4. Route. Per contractor, validate the beneficiary against the country schema and set LOCAL vs SWIFT explicitly from fee and a per-country cut-off table. If a cut-off is about to be missed, re-route or escalate.
  5. Protect. A bank-detail change within N days of payday, or an instruction that arrives by email or chat, holds that single payout and triggers out-of-band verification. The rest of payroll proceeds.
  6. Recover. A returned transfer (CANCELLED with a bank-return failure_type) triggers re-verification and a re-pay with a new request_id under a duplicate lock, so nobody is paid twice and the original is reconciled.
  7. Collect. Charge the platform fee from each employer with charges/create, only after that employer's payroll has settled.
  8. Fallback (Metal add-on). Contractors who opted in can receive an instant USDZ payout on Metal testnet, under a spending-limited agent mandate, when every fiat rail would miss payday.

Safety by design: the model reads, the code decides, the API moves the money

  • The LLM (Claude, typed tool calls only) reads unstructured inputs: employer emails ("funds coming Thursday"), bank return notices and contractor messages. It proposes a plan and explains exceptions in plain language.
  • Deterministic, unit-tested policy code decides every amount, priority, limit, tenant check, cut-off and fraud hold. The model never chooses an amount and never sees credentials. Instructions found inside emails are treated as data, so the diversion email doubles as a prompt-injection test.
  • Every human approval is bound to a hash of the exact amount, currency, beneficiary, employer account and evidence. Any change invalidates it.
  • Idempotent request_ids on every mutating call, ambiguous responses are reconciled before any retry, and sandbox simulations sit behind one interface.

Demo (under 5 minutes, all sandbox, synthetic data)

The demo follows Goal → Plan → New information → Revised decision → Result. A platform pays 3 employers × 12 contractors in the US, UK, DE, MX, PH and IN on Friday.

  1. Plan. A world map of paydays. Live balances per employer, net FX gap incl. fees, and a rail per contractor.
  2. New info #1: short funding. Employer B is funded at 60%, and a deposit lands mid-run in Employer C's wallet. A naive agent would "see enough money". Payday Guardian pays B's priority wages, holds the rest, drafts B's funding request, and never touches A's or C's money.
  3. New info #2: diversion email. "Please send my pay to this new account today, urgent." Only Maria's payout is held and flagged as social engineering. The other 35 go out.
  4. New info #3: return. One MXN transfer goes SENT → FAILED (bank return). The agent re-verifies and re-pays once, without a duplicate.
  5. New info #4: cut-off risk. The PHP cut-off is at risk. The agent re-routes, or for an opted-in contractor pays instantly on Metal.
  6. Result. Final balances read back from Airwallex, platform fees collected, and a payday report: on-time %, FX/fees saved by netting, fraud held, escalations, and a full audit log of every API call.

Airwallex APIs used

Authentication · Connected Accounts (create/submit/activate, one per employer) · x-on-behalf-of on every employer call · Global Accounts + deposit simulation · Balances · FX rates, quotes & conversions · Beneficiary schema / validate / create · Transfers (LOCAL & SWIFT) + transfer status simulation · charges/create (platform fee) · connected_account_transfers (only with explicit approval, never as a silent cross-tenant bridge) · Webhooks (HMAC-verified) · Platform reports for reconciliation. Optional: Metal testnet mandates and direct payouts. Build tooling: Airwallex Docs and Developer MCP servers.

Why it matters / why Airwallex

Paying people across borders on time is where Airwallex's local payout rails in 150+ countries, multi-currency Global Accounts and FX matter most, and it is a core Airwallex use case (EOR / payroll aggregator payouts). Payday Guardian turns that infrastructure into an autonomous, auditable payroll operator for any payroll, EOR or gig platform. It is tenant-isolated, approval-bound, fraud-aware and idempotent by construction.

Tech stack

TypeScript/Node, Airwallex sandbox REST API (MCP for docs and payload assembly), Claude for document reading and explanations (typed tools only), policy engine as pure tested functions, a small web console (Goal → Plan → New information → Revised decision → Result) with a payday map, and a SQLite audit log.

Loop engineering log (4 iterations)

  • Loop 1: generate and screen. We considered 7 directions: treasury controller, intent-bound SaaS purchase, dispute agent, FX-netting AR/AP, x402 inference sourcing, zero-day close, and multi-employer payroll. We scored each on originality, Airwallex fit, demo drama, safety story, buildability, and sponsor-award reach. Public GitHub showed Kits 1, 2 and 4 already crowded with clones, while payroll (Kit 6) was under-served. Its platform-access requirement deters casual entrants.
  • Loop 2: critique the top 3. FX-netting had strong Airwallex fit but read as a Kit 1 clone. x402 sourcing had a sponsor angle but is trendy and likely crowded. Payroll scored highest on business pain, on showcasing Airwallex's moat (local rails, cut-offs, FX) and on emotional demo value ("everyone gets paid"). Weakness: a plain "pay contractors" flow is a thin decision.
  • Loop 3: harden the winner. We made it a real multi-decision agent: tenant isolation, cut-off-aware routing, net FX, partial-funding priority and returns recovery. We added an adversarial moment (payroll-diversion email that doubles as a prompt-injection test), the "model reads / code decides / API moves money" rule, hash-bound approvals, and a Metal fallback rail for the Metal Award.
  • Loop 4: red-team against the official Kit 6 guide. We checked every step against the builder guide's sandbox constraints and added what judges will look for. Fees are priced before converting. The mid-run deposit lands in the wrong tenant (the cross-tenant summing trap). x-on-behalf-of is on every employer call, with read-back confirmation. Rails are set explicitly. Returns are modelled as SENT → FAILED with a failure_type. The platform fee is collected via charges/create. Platform access is requested up front. Result: same idea, fewer demo-day failure points.
